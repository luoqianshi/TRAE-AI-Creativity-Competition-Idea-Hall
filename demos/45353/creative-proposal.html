02.1 How Perception Works

Perception doesn't search — it understands. It reads your query, extracts intent, and assembles a response from multiple knowledge domains as a coherent editorial experience.

01

Intent Classification

The system first analyzes your query using an enhanced keyword extraction engine that goes beyond simple pattern matching. It identifies functional intent (are you looking for information, tools, or inspiration?) and content domain (design, technology, culture, and more) through a layered classification system that understands both explicit and implicit signals.

02

Multimodal Retrieval

Perception simultaneously queries multiple content sources — visual inspiration libraries, structured knowledge bases, real-time data feeds, and personal archives — using domain-optimized retrieval strategies. Images are matched by visual similarity and semantic content, not just metadata tags. Articles are ranked by editorial quality and relevance depth.

03

Semantic Synthesis

Retrieved content passes through a synthesis layer that identifies connections, surfaces contradictions, and groups results by thematic coherence rather than source type. This is where Perception distinguishes itself: it doesn't just aggregate — it assembles a narrative, presenting information as a curated editorial experience with natural flow and hierarchical structure.

04

Contextual Presentation

Results are rendered as composable modules — image grids, text excerpts, data visualizations, functional widgets — arranged in an editorial layout that respects content hierarchy. The presentation adapts to your device, your history, and the nature of the query, ensuring information density is always matched to comprehension capacity.

Design Principles

Six foundational principles that guide every design decision — from micro-interactions to system architecture.

03.1 Core Principles

Design is not decoration — it's a philosophy made visible. Every element should earn its place on the screen.

01

Clarity over Density

We prioritize comprehension over information display. An interface that shows everything at once shows nothing effectively. Perception uses progressive disclosure — revealing detail as needed, maintaining focus on what matters in each moment.

02

Calm over Stimulation

Intelligence tools should reduce cognitive load, not add to it. Perception employs warm neutral palettes, generous whitespace, and measured animations. Nothing shouts. Everything respects the user's attention as their most precious resource.

03

Craft over Convenience

Every typographic choice, every spacing unit, every animation curve is deliberate. We believe quality is felt before it's named. The interface should feel like a well-made object — considered, balanced, and worthy of repeated use.

04

Depth over Surface

We build systems that invite exploration rather than demand it. Surface simplicity reveals underlying richness through interaction. Each layer of depth is earned through curiosity, creating a sense of discovery without overwhelming the first encounter.

05

Human over Technical

Technology should feel invisible when it works well. Perception speaks natural language, respects human rhythm, and adapts to human needs rather than the other way around. The system serves the person, never the reverse.

06

Timeless over Trendy

We design for years, not seasons. Our visual language draws from editorial traditions that have endured for decades — thoughtful typography, structured grids, restrained palettes. Perception should feel just as fresh on its thousandth use as on its first.

The best interface is the one you don't notice — until it does something so thoughtful, so aligned with your intent, that you pause and feel understood. That's the Perception standard.

Interaction Model

A conversational interface that feels natural from the first interaction — asking questions, receiving editorial responses, and refining through continuous dialogue.

04.1 The Input Layer

Speak naturally. Perception handles the translation. There are no special commands to learn, no syntax to remember.

Natural Language Ask questions, describe needs, or think out loud. Perception extracts intent from casual speech.
Image Input Drop an image to find similar ones, identify styles, or explore related visual references.
Context Carry Follow-up questions maintain context. "Show me more like that" works without re-explaining.

04.2 The Response Layer

Responses are composed, not just displayed. Perception assembles modular content cards into editorial layouts that match the nature of each query.

A

Visual Inspiration Modules

Image-heavy queries return curated galleries with editorial layout — hero images, supporting grids, and detail views. Hover states reveal metadata without breaking the visual flow. Images are presented as compositions, not thumbnails.

B

Knowledge Synthesis Modules

Informational queries receive structured, readable responses — clear hierarchies, pull-quote callouts, and progressive detail layers. Sources are cited but unobtrusive, available when you need them but never distracting from understanding.

C

Functional Widget Modules

Practical queries — weather, calculations, calendar checks — return focused, actionable widgets. Just enough information, immediately useful, with paths to deeper context if you want it. No unnecessary chrome, no wasted motion.

D

Collection & Archive Modules

Save anything to your personal knowledge base with a single action. Collections build automatically around themes you return to. Over time, Perception knows your archive well enough to answer questions about your own saved work.

System Architecture

A modular, extensible architecture designed for growth — from personal intelligence system to platform-scale knowledge infrastructure.

05.1 Architectural Layers

Five layers, each with a single responsibility, working together to create an experience greater than the sum of their parts.

01

Interface Layer

The presentation layer that renders modular responses as editorial experiences. Handles layout composition, responsive adaptation, animation orchestration, and user interaction capture. Built with React and Framer Motion for fluid, deliberate motion that guides attention without demanding it.

02

Orchestration Layer

The conversational state manager that tracks context across interactions, manages multi-turn dialogue, and decides which capabilities to invoke for each query. This is where intent becomes action — routing requests to the right services and assembling results into coherent responses.

03

Intelligence Layer

The semantic understanding core — keyword classification, intent recognition, content scoring, and personalization. This layer processes both the query and the user's history to determine not just what to show, but why and how. It's the heart of Perception's understanding capability.

04

Content Layer

Pluggable content sources — visual inspiration libraries, RSS feeds, news APIs, weather services, image databases, personal notes, and more. Each source adapter normalizes content to a common format, making the system endlessly extensible without changing its core.

05

Storage Layer

Personal knowledge persistence — saved items, collections, preference profiles, interaction history, and taste signals. This layer grows richer with every use, forming a personal knowledge graph that makes Perception increasingly valuable over time.
