IntroductionCommon beginner problem

Many students lose marks before they lose language.

“I know words, but when the examiner asks me, my mind goes blank.”

Why this happens

  • Students do not know the test rhythm.
  • They try to translate a perfect answer first.
  • They answer too shortly because they fear mistakes.
Better goal: answer clearly, extend simply, and keep speaking even if the sentence is not perfect.

Test Snapshot11–14 minutes

IELTS Speaking is a short face-to-face interview with three parts.

1

Part 1

Short questions about you and familiar topics.

Usually 4–5 minutes.

2

Part 2

One topic card. Prepare for 1 minute, then speak for up to 2 minutes.

Also called the “long turn”.

3

Part 3

Discussion questions connected to Part 2, but more abstract.

Usually 4–5 minutes.

✓

One Score

All three parts are used to decide your speaking band score.

No separate Part 1/2/3 score.

Format first, strategy second

IntroductionWhat is tested?

The examiner is checking communication, not your personality or special knowledge.

What matters

  • Can you answer the question?
  • Can you keep the conversation going?
  • Can you explain ideas with enough language?
  • Can the examiner understand your pronunciation?

What does not matter

  • Being funny, clever, or impressive.
  • Having an unusual life story.
  • Using extremely difficult words everywhere.
  • Having a native-speaker accent.
Simple truth: a clear ordinary answer is better than a confused “advanced” answer.
Communication over performance

Format & FeaturesPart 1 · familiar topics

Part 1 is warm-up conversation, but your answers still need development.

Typical topics

  • Work or study
  • Hometown and home
  • Food, music, sports, weekends
  • Daily habits and preferences

Good answer shape

  • Direct answer
  • One reason
  • One small detail or example
Good: “Yes, I enjoy cooking because it helps me relax. I usually make simple dishes like tomato eggs after class.”
Too short: “Yes, I like cooking.”
Part 1: answer + reason + detail

Format & FeaturesPart 2 · long turn

Part 2 is not a memory test. It is a two-minute speaking task.

Describe a useful app you often use.

You should say what it is, how you use it, why it is useful, and explain how it affects your daily life.

Use your 1 minute well

  • Write keywords, not full sentences.
  • Plan 3–4 points in order.
  • Prepare one example and one feeling.
Simple structure: what it is → when I use it → one example → why it helps me → final opinion.
Part 2: organize before speaking

Format & FeaturesImportant rules

The test feels natural, but it follows strict timing and question rules.

Recorded

Your speaking test is recorded. This protects both the candidate and the examiner.

Examiner-led

The examiner asks the questions and controls the timing. You do not choose the topic.

No specialist knowledge

Questions are designed for general communication, not academic expertise.

You may ask for repetition: “Sorry, could you repeat the question?” But do not ask the examiner to explain every word in detail.
Natural conversation, formal assessment

MarksFluency & coherence

Fluency does not mean speaking fast. It means speaking with a clear flow.

Better: “I usually study in the evening because it is quieter. For example, after dinner I review vocabulary for about twenty minutes.”
Weaker: “I study evening. Quiet. Vocabulary. Good.”

Use connectors

because, for example, also, but, so

Avoid long silence

Use thinking phrases: “Let me think for a second.”

Stay on topic

Answer the exact question first, then add detail.

Flow, links, and relevance

MarksGrammar range & accuracy

Grammar is about control. Use simple sentences correctly, then add variety.

Simple sentence

“I live in a small city.”

Reason clause

“I like it because it is quiet.”

Time contrast

“When I was younger, I preferred noisy places.”

Better: “I have studied English for many years, but I still need more speaking practice.”
Weak control: “I study English many years but speaking still not very good.”
Accuracy first, variety next

MarksPronunciation

You do not need a British or American accent. You need to be easy to understand.

Sounds

Can the key words be heard clearly?

Word stress

Say “con-VEN-ient”, not every syllable equally.

Sentence stress

Emphasize meaning words: “I usually study at NIGHT.”

Chunking

Pause in groups, not after every single word.

Practice line: “I usually study at night, because it is quieter and I can focus better.”
Clear beats “native-like”

Test AdviceSimple answer formula

For most beginner answers, use: answer, reason, example, feeling.

1. Answer

Give a direct response first.

2. Reason

Explain why with “because”.

3. Example

Add a small real-life detail.

4. Feeling

End with your opinion or feeling.

Question: Do you like reading?
Answer: Yes, I do. I like reading because it helps me relax after a busy day. For example, I often read short stories before I sleep. It makes me feel calm.
A-R-E-F: Answer · Reason · Example · Feeling

Test AdviceDuring the test

Small habits can make a big difference in speaking performance.

Do

  • Answer the question first.
  • Speak in complete short sentences.
  • Use examples from daily life.
  • Correct yourself naturally if needed.

Do not

  • Memorize long model answers.
  • Use difficult words you cannot control.
  • Stop completely after one mistake.
  • Give only “yes” or “no”.
Important: memorized answers often sound unnatural. Examiners can notice when the answer does not match the question.
Be clear, not scripted

Mini PracticeTry it now

Use the beginner formula to answer one Part 1 question.

“What do you usually do on weekends?”

30-second preparation

  • Direct answer: usually / sometimes / not often
  • Reason: because...
  • Example: last weekend / with my friends / at home
  • Feeling: relaxing / fun / a little boring
Model: “I usually stay at home on weekends because I feel tired after school. For example, I watch films or clean my room on Saturday afternoon. It is simple, but it helps me recharge.”
Practice structure, then speed

ReviewBefore next lesson

If you remember these five points, you understand the test overview.

1. The test has three parts.

Part 1: familiar questions. Part 2: long turn. Part 3: discussion.

2. You are marked in four equal areas.

Fluency, vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation.

3. Clear answers are better than memorized answers.

Use simple structure: answer, reason, example, feeling.

4. Mistakes are normal.

Keep speaking, repair naturally, and focus on communication.
